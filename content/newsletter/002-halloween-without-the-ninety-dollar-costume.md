Halloween is the first holiday of the season where the stores get to set your budget if you let them. This week's tactic: **pick the number before you walk into the costume aisle.**

## The $40 night

This is a family of four, two trick-or-treaters. Adjust up or down, but write it down first.

- **Costumes: $20 total.** One store-bought, one built from the closet. Kids under six do not know the difference between a licensed costume and a cardboard box with a label on it.
- **Candy to hand out: $15.** One bag. When it runs out, porch light off. That is the whole system.
- **Pumpkin: $5.** One. Everyone gets a turn with the scoop.

If there is a school party or a trunk-or-treat, that comes out of the same $40. That's the part people forget.

## The closet costume

Every house already has three costumes in it. Look for:

- A flannel and a beanie (lumberjack)
- Any solid sweatsuit plus paper ears (cat, bear, whatever color the sweatsuit is)
- A white sheet you were going to replace anyway

Your kid gets to pick, you get to say "we're building it." That sentence does more work than a credit card.

## When the kid wants the $60 one

They will. Say what's true: "That one's not in our Halloween number. Want to pick from these three, or help me build one?" Two choices, both yours.

If they still want it, it can go on the birthday or holiday list. Not a no. A not-today.
