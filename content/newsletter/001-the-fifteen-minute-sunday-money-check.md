Most money stress is not one big number. It is the feeling of not knowing the number.

So here is the whole tactic for this week: **fifteen minutes, Sunday morning, before anyone asks you for anything.**

## The three questions

Open the bank app. Not the budgeting app you set up in January and abandoned. The actual bank.

1. **What landed since last Sunday?** Paychecks, refunds, the $40 your brother finally paid back.
2. **What left that I did not plan?** Circle anything under $20 at a gas station, drive-thru, or delivery app. Those are not moral failures. They are signs you ran out of food or time.
3. **What is due before next Sunday?** Bills, school fees, the birthday party on Saturday that needs a gift.

Write the answers on paper. Three lines. That's the meeting.

## Why Sunday

Monday is reactive. By Wednesday you are guessing. Sunday is the one window where the week has not started arguing with you yet.

If you share money with another adult, do this together once a month. Not every week. Weekly joint money meetings are how couples learn to hate each other over the price of string cheese.

## When it goes sideways

Some Sundays the answer to question two is ugly. Five drive-thru charges. A $68 Amazon order nobody remembers placing.

Do not spend the fifteen minutes on blame. Spend one minute on the upstream fix: was there food in the house? Was there a plan for Tuesday dinner? Fix that and the small charges usually fix themselves.

See you next Sunday.
