Fall seasons are underway, which means the gear emails are coming. Cleats, bags, the "recommended" shin guards, the team-branded water bottle. This week's tactic: **set the gear cutoff before the season sets it for you.**

## The gear cutoff

Two numbers, decided before the first practice:

1. **A dollar cap for gear this season.** Write it down. Share it with the other adult in the house.
2. **A date after which nothing new gets bought.** Usually two weeks in. If something breaks after that, it gets fixed, borrowed, or waits.

The cap is not about being cheap. It is about not finding out in November that the "small" purchases added up to more than the registration fee. If you have not run the full number yet, use the [youth sports all-in cost checklist](/guides/youth-sports-all-in-cost-checklist) first.

## Wait two weeks

Kids quit things. That is not a parenting failure, it is being eight. Buy the minimum to get through the first two weeks of practice: borrowed cleats, a water bottle from the cabinet, whatever bag is in the closet.

If they are still excited at week two, buy the real stuff. If they are not, you just saved the money you would have spent on gear for a sport they did not want.

Older siblings, team swaps, and the parent group chat are all real sources. Ask before you buy. Someone always has a bag of outgrown cleats in the garage.

## Bare tool pricing

Different aisle, same idea. If you already own one cordless tool, you own the most expensive part of every other tool on that platform: the battery.

Look for "tool only" or "bare tool" on the tag. The kit version of the same tool includes a battery and charger you already have, and that is a big part of the price. Over a few tools, the difference adds up to real money.

The rule: **pick one battery platform and stay on it.** Your second, third, and fourth tools should all be bare.

## When this breaks

The coach sends a "required" gear list after your cutoff date. Ask whether it is required or recommended. Ask whether the team has loaners. Then buy only what is actually required, and only once.

If the sport is clearly not going to fit, [what you cut so the kid can keep playing](/guides/what-you-cut-so-the-kid-can-keep-playing-sports) walks through the trade-offs without making anyone feel bad about them.
