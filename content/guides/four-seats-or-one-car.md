Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. The group text is already about who is coming. The number to put on the table this week is seats, not stuffing.

You do not need a speech about family. You need one plan you can text before the thread picks the pricey one.

## Four seats is a second bill

Hopper told CBS News on September 28, 2026 that the average domestic round trip over Thanksgiving is **$402**, up $95 from last year. Four of those is **$1,608**. That is before a checked bag, a ride from the airport, or the sandwiches you buy because the flight is late.

> Remember: Four seats at $402 is $1,608 before a bag.

That average is not your quote. Your airport can be worse, or a little kinder. It is still the right size for the decision. Christmas was $452 in the same Hopper note. Leave it out of this text.

## Sunday is the expensive seat

Hopper economist Hayley Berg told CBS that Sunday, November 29, runs about **$250 more per round-trip ticket** than a return on another day. On four seats, that gap is about $1,000. She said to fly out Monday or Tuesday, November 23 or 24, instead of Wednesday, when the airports fill up. For the way home she named Black Friday, Saturday morning, Monday, or Tuesday. Any day but that Sunday.

If the kids still have school on Tuesday, you do not have to pull them to chase the cheapest outbound. Wednesday out and Saturday back still dodges the Sunday hit. The lever is the day, not a lecture about being flexible.

> Truth: The cheap return is any day but Sunday.

> Say: "We're not on the Sunday flight. That's the expensive seat."

Berg also told people to book before Halloween, because she did not expect fares to get kinder. Halloween was Saturday. On November 3, that window is closed. Waiting is not a strategy. Headcount and the return day still are.

## Two tickets is a real plan

Someone still wants the visit. Flip who moves. Two adult tickets at the same average are **$804**. That is still real money. It is not four seats, four bags, and a car seat negotiation at the gate.

Grandparents can often fly Tuesday and come back Saturday. You cannot pull a first-grader out of a week of school to save the same $250. Let the people with a free calendar take the cheaper days.

> To your folks: "Two tickets to us is the trip we can do."

If they cannot travel, you are back to four seats or the car. That is a choice. It is not a failed holiday. A short, kind no already has a script in [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go). Thanksgiving gets the short version.

> Say: "We can't do four seats this year. We can do two, or we can drive."

## The car is tank math, not a vibe

AAA listed the national average for regular at **$4.25 a gallon** on October 28, 2026. The figure on the page that day was $4.247. At 25 miles a gallon, every 100 miles of the round trip is 4 gallons, about $17. A 1,000-mile round trip is 40 gallons, about **$170**.

That $170 is gas only. A hotel on the way, tolls, and a drive-through can pass a cheap pair of tickets. Write the miles before you announce the drive. If the map says two days each way, price the beds out loud. If it says one long day, the car is usually the cheaper seat.

> Say: "We're driving. I wrote the miles down."

Hosting is the other cheap seat. Dinner for the people already in the house is a grocery week, not a fare. The pair-the-nights plan is in [the $47 grocery week](/guides/the-47-dollar-grocery-week). If they stay over, the heat runs. That bill already has a move in [the heat bill before it gets cold](/guides/the-heat-bill-before-it-gets-cold). A full house is still cheaper than four Sunday seats. The wider pattern, small asks that turn into a real bill, is the [dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax). Four plane seats are this month's version.

## Do it this week

Write three lines. Text one of them. Do not text all three and let the group pick the pricey one.

- Two tickets: they fly to you, on a day that is not Sunday.
- One car: the miles, the gas, and a hotel if the map says you need one.
- Four seats: only if the return is Black Friday, Saturday, Monday, or Tuesday.

> To your kid: "We're doing Thanksgiving here. Or we drive. Not four plane seats."

## The point

Broke does not mean broken. The table can be your kitchen. The visit can be two people on a Tuesday flight. The car can be the plan when the miles are short enough to say out loud. Four Sunday seats are how a Thursday dinner turns into a bill you still feel in January.

> Remember: Two tickets or one car. Not four seats on the expensive day.
