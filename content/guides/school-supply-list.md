August has a special tax. It arrives as a cheerful PDF from the school and ends as a cart full of things you swear you already bought last year and somehow no longer own.

You are not disorganized. The list is designed to assume infinite storage, infinite markers, and a parent who can drop $120 without blinking.

## Triage the list

Make three columns on a scrap of paper:

1. **Must:** teacher will notice; kid cannot function without it (folder, pencils, the one notebook they named).
2. **Share / reuse:** last year's scissors, backpack if it still zips, headphones that still work.
3. **Skip or delay:** decorative folders, brand-name everything, the third set of markers, "optional" donations dressed as requirements.

If the school truly requires something exotic, buy that. Everything else is negotiable until proven otherwise.

## Where to buy without bleeding

- What you already have at home (inventory first, fifteen minutes saves forty dollars)
- Dollar aisle / store brand for consumables
- One big-box run with a hard number written on your phone before you enter
- Ask the teacher, quietly, if generic is fine. It almost always is.

Do not shop hungry, angry, or with a kid who has veto power over pencil grip aesthetics.

## The donation ask

Classroom "wish lists" are real needs and also an open faucet. Pick one item you can actually give. Skip the rest without a speech. You are not a bad community member for protecting August's grocery money.

## Scripts

- To your kid: "We're buying the list, not the whole aisle."
- To another parent: "We're keeping it lean this year."
- To yourself: "This is a season. It is not a referendum on my love."

## What not to do

Do not buy the "fun" version of every item to compensate for guilt. Do not wait until 9 p.m. the night before, that is how you pay convenience prices. Do not pretend the list is optional if your kid will be embarrassed; solve the musts, ignore the theater.

## The point

School should not require a side hustle to open a locker. Do the musts. Reuse what you can. Leave the rest on the shelf. Broke does not mean broken, and a kid with a plain folder still gets to learn.
