Friday, November 20, is the last school morning before a lot of Thanksgiving breaks. The flyer is already in the folder. Donuts with Dads. Dudes at the Door. It says bring your energy.

A donut with your kid before the bell is a good morning. The part that wrecks the day is treating that donut like a shift: a dance tunnel, a Thursday night bag assembly, or a clock-in you cannot move.

## What a short one looks like

The breakfasts that stayed a visit were about 45 minutes, and they ended when school started.

Windermere Elementary in Florida ran Donuts with Dads on November 12 and November 13, 2026, from 7:45 to 8:30 a.m. in the cafeteria. Adults RSVP by name. You do not add your kid to the count. If the office has never scanned your ID, you check in there first. Everyone else goes straight to the cafeteria. Coffee for the dads. Then the bell.

Indian Trail Middle School in Plainfield, Illinois, posted Dudes at the Door for Friday, November 20. Arrive at 7:00 with your kid for a donut. At 7:30 the dads go outside. School starts at 7:55, and the post says dads are free to go after that.

That is the visit. Fifty-five minutes, then your actual job.

> Remember: The donut ends at the bell.

## The tunnel is a show

Indian Trail's 7:30 block is two tunnels of dads, dancing and high-fiving kids into the building, then down the hall until study hall. The signup asks you to bring your energy, smiles, and dance moves.

A donut at a table is you and your kid. A tunnel is a show for the other kids, with you in the cast. If you want that, stay. If you do not, you still showed up.

Eat the donut. Walk your kid to the door. Leave when the bell rings.

> Say: "I'll do the donut. I have to leave at the bell."

> Truth: A dance tunnel is a show. Drop-off is the visit.

Same shape as a class party. [The class party signup is one job](/guides/the-class-party-signup-is-one-job): one slot, not the whole afternoon. This is one donut, not the entertainment committee.

## Thursday night is a different signup

Chesapeake Math and IT Academy North in Laurel, Maryland, posted Donuts with Dads for Friday, November 20, with a window from 7:55 to 9:55 a.m. The volunteer form beside it is a separate job: Thursday, November 19, 4:00 to 6:00 p.m., to decorate and assemble classroom bags, and Friday, 7:15 a.m. to 10:00 a.m., for check-in, ushering, and classroom support.

The Friday block ends about two hours after a normal drop-off. The Thursday block is after work and before dinner. Neither one is the donut.

> Truth: Thursday night bag duty is a shift.

> Say: "I can come for the donut. I can't do the Thursday setup."

Watch the extra errand too. Boone Elementary in Dallas ran a November 13 breakfast from 7:15 to 8:00 a.m. and also collected coats and jeans. Donations had to be bagged. A coat you already own is a fine add. A second store run the night before is the shift again. One bag, or no bag. The donut does not depend on it.

## If the morning is already spoken for

Some of these start before your shift. Hirayama Elementary in Fresno posted Donuts with Dad for November 10, from 6:45 to 7:45 a.m., and sent people to RevTrak to register. Indian Trail wants you at 7:00 on November 20. If you are on a clock at 7, skip the breakfast.

Tell your kid the night before. One line. No speech about the dads who can make it, and no speech about money.

> To your kid: "I can't come that morning. You still walk in with your class."

If you can move one hour and you want the donut, go, and leave at the bell. Do not burn a half day so you can high-five in a hallway until 10.

> Say: "I can't make Friday morning. I'll see you at pickup."

You do not owe the room a reason. If a shorter no is hard to find, the lines in [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go) work for a school breakfast too.

## Say the plan in the car

If you are going, the bell should not be a surprise.

> To your kid: "Donut, then I go to work. That's the plan."

## Reply before the form grows

Do this before you type "happy to help with whatever."

- The window ends when school starts: go if you can. Leave at the bell.
- The form asks for a tunnel, a Thursday setup, or a morning until 10: take the donut, or take none of it.
- A coat drive is one bag you already own, or a no.
- You cannot move your clock: one line the night before. No speech.

> Say: "Donut and drop-off. I'm not staying for the tunnel."

## The point

Broke does not mean broken. A cafeteria donut before the bell counts. The tunnel, the Thursday bags, and the morning that runs until 10 are how a nice flyer becomes a shift you did not apply for.

> Remember: Eat the donut. Leave at the bell.
