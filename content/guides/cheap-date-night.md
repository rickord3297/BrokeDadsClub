Date night dies in the logistics. The second you start pricing a sitter, parking, and a restaurant that takes adults seriously, the couch wins.

Keep the kids. Change the rules. Same calm energy as [explaining “we can’t go”](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go): one honest plan beats an expensive apology.

## Is tonight a date night or a sleep night?

Both are legitimate. Picking wrong is how you end up annoyed at each other over a candle.

Signs it is a date night: every conversation for four days has been a handoff ("did you get her," "who's driving Thursday"), and you are irritated at each other without being able to name why. That is usually attention starvation, and 90 minutes fixes more of it than you would think.

Signs it is a sleep night: one of you has been out cold on the couch by 8:40 three nights running, or somebody is sick, or tomorrow starts at 4:30. Take the sleep and put an actual date on the calendar with a day attached. "Soon" is not a day.

> Remember: Attention is the whole product. Everything else is set dressing.

## The 90-minute home date

1. **Announce it.** Give the kids the shape of the night before you clear the plates, not after.
2. **Close the kitchen.** Paper plates if that is what it takes. No loading the dishwasher "real quick."
3. **Eat something you would not eat on a Tuesday.** Frozen pizza dressed up with extra cheese still counts if you light a candle and put your phones in a bowl.
4. **Ask one question that is not about the calendar.** "What did you used to want to be?" is better than "Did you email the preschool?"

The announcement, out loud:

> "After dinner, it's grown-up time. You get a movie / extra story / the cheap popcorn. We get the table."

## Set the kids up so they do not need you

Interruptions almost always come from three things: hunger, a screen that needs an adult, and not knowing when the night ends. Handle all three before you sit down.

- Snacks portioned and already on the counter. A kid who has to ask is a kid who is at your table.
- The movie is queued and playing. Not "we'll find something."
- Give them a visible ending. "When the movie ends" or a timer they can see. Vague endings produce a check-in every eight minutes.
- Water, bathroom, blanket, lights. Cheap insurance.
- Baby in the house? The date happens next to the monitor. Monitor on the table, phones still in the bowl.

## When they revolt anyway

Plan for one interruption so it costs forty seconds instead of the night.

- **First appearance:** handle it standing in the doorway. Do not sit down with them. Sitting down ends the date.
- **Second appearance inside five minutes:** one of you goes and resettles them completely. Bathroom, water, tuck, done. Only one adult leaves. Two adults leaving the table is how date night quietly becomes bedtime, and bedtime becomes scrolling.
- **Third appearance:** call it and reschedule out loud. "Tomorrow, same time" turns a bust into a postponement instead of a verdict on your marriage.

If the whole thing shrinks to 25 minutes, take the 25. A short date that happened beats a long one you keep pushing to a weekend that never clears.

If you run out of things to say in twelve minutes, that is not a diagnosis. It means you have been running a shift together instead of talking. Ask about something from before the kids and let it be a little awkward for a minute.

## If you can leave the house for $20

Walk a grocery store like it is a museum. Split a bakery item in the parking lot. Drive to the place with the good sunset and sit in the car like teenagers with a better credit score (still not great).

Leaving still needs coverage, so trade for it instead of buying it. Find one family at the same stage and swap Friday nights: they take your kids this week, you take theirs next week. Set it as a standing swap or it will never actually happen.

The point is not the venue. The point is a slice of time that is not a staff meeting about children.

## When this breaks

**One of you is too fried to want it.** The tell is a yes with no eye contact, or "sure, if you want to." Do not push it into a performance. Cut it to thirty minutes on the porch with no candle and no plan, or trade it straight for a sleep night and book the real one. A date night somebody got dragged into becomes a thing they hold against you later.

**Your nights never overlap.** Shift work, a second job, a nursing schedule. Stop trying to force an evening. Take the 40 minutes that already exist: coffee before the house wakes up, the gap between one shift ending and the other starting, a Sunday morning in the car. Same rules travel. Phones down, kitchen closed, no logistics.

## What not to do

Do not turn it into a budget lecture. Do not "use the time" to talk about the leaking faucet. Put the faucet on a note for Sunday. Tonight you are two people who picked each other, even if the baby monitor is on the table.

One exception: if there is a real thing sitting under the table (money, a fight you never finished), a candle will not outrun it. Give it twenty minutes at the start, on purpose, then close it and move on. Pretending is more expensive than talking.
