Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. The shopping guides did not wait. On November 14, USA Today ran a teacher-gift list: a DoorDash card, an Amazon card, a notebook, and an Owala tumbler from Target's teacher shop. The same piece says $15 to $30 is a useful range for one teacher.

You are not late. Break is next week. A lot of schools send kids home early on Wednesday, November 25, so anything that has to ride in a backpack needs to be in there before that morning. The thing that has to ride is a card. The tumbler can stay on the website.

> Remember: The card is the thank-you. December is still a gift month.

## What the list is actually selling

USA Today's November 14, 2026 piece says DoorDash sells eGift cards from $5 to $500. Amazon gift cards on that same page run $10 to $50, or a 3-pack of $10 cards if you have more than one teacher. A lined notebook on the list was under $30. The spend advice at the bottom is $15 to $30 for one teacher, and a group gift if several parents want to split a bigger card.

They also point at Target's 2026 teacher shop and call out an Owala FreeSip. Walmart.com, checked November 14, 2026, listed the FreeSip 24 oz at $29.97. That is the top of the range they just called useful, for a bottle the teacher may already own.

The $5 card and the $30 bottle are on the same menu. The article even says a small gift with a sincere note can feel as thoughtful as something expensive. Take them at the second half of that sentence.

> Truth: Five dollars is a card. Thirty dollars is a second Christmas.

## The card goes in the bag

You do not need a boxed set. A piece of notebook paper folded once is a card. If a box is already in a drawer, use one of those. Do not make a trip for stationery the week the grocery list is already doing enough.

Ask for one sentence, not a speech.

> To your kid: "Write one thing you like at school. I'll put it in your bag."

If they freeze, give them a fill-in. The lunch. The read-aloud. The fact that the teacher knows their name. You can write the words they say and let them sign it. That still counts. A perfect card written by a tired adult does not land harder than a crooked sentence.

Put it in the backpack Sunday or Monday. Wednesday is the early day at a lot of schools, and Thursday is the holiday. A card that misses the bag this week is not a failure. It becomes the December note. It does not become a reason to order a tumbler tonight.

> Say: "The card goes in Monday. That's the thank-you."

## If the text wants money

A room parent may already be collecting for December. That collection is the gift. USA Today says a group gift keeps one family from covering a bigger card alone, and that you should check the school rules before you send cash or a gift card. Some districts cap gifts or ban cash. The short reply still works if the answer is no.

> To the room parent: "We'll do the December collection. Not a second gift this week."

If there is no December plan and you want the thank-you to be spendable, stop at DoorDash's $5 card or the $10 Amazon card from that same article. One of them. Not both, and not a bottle beside them. Two teachers means two cards or the 3-pack of $10 Amazon cards, not two tumblers.

This is the same shape as the class party sheet. One job, then stop. That plan is in [the class party signup is one job](/guides/the-class-party-signup-is-one-job). A clipboard that wants your whole week is the huddle problem in [don't say yes to team parent in the first huddle](/guides/dont-say-yes-to-team-parent-first-huddle).

If the number stings, the line is a no, not a budget speech. [Explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go) is the same muscle. The [dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax) is the pile these $15 asks join.

The kid's own present is a different list. One name, then close the ad: [the list is one name](/guides/the-list-is-one-name). A teacher bottle does not come out of that name, and that name does not become a teacher gift.

> Remember: One thank-you. Not a November gift and a December gift.

## What you tell your kid

Somebody in class will show up with a bag. Your kid will see it.

> To your kid: "Your teacher gets a note from you. That's the gift."

If you added the $5 card, say that in the same breath. Do not apologize for the size. A kid can hear "we don't do the bottle" without hearing "we are behind."

> Say: "Other families can bring more. We brought a note."

## The point

Broke doesn't mean broken. Write the sentence this weekend. Slide it into the backpack. Leave the tumbler in the tab. December can have one gift if you still want one. This week has a card.
