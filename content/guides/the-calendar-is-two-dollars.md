Thanksgiving dinner already has a list. The week of November 4, the same kind of store put a second wall next to it.

Aldi Reviewer updated its readout of the weekly ad on October 30, 2026. In most areas that ad starts Wednesday, November 4. Advent calendars are the big Find. The Choceur kids chocolate calendar is $1.99 for 2.29 ounces. The LEGO City or Friends calendar on the same list is $34.99. One of those is a countdown. The other is a toy.

## The wall is two prices

The $1.99 box is small. A little over two ounces of chocolate, assorted designs, one door for each day you decide to open. That is a calendar.

The rest of that list is toys with doors on the front:

- Fisher-Price Little People, $26.99, Santa's workshop or Disney Princesses
- Squishmallow 12-day, $29.99
- Monster Jam, $24.99
- LEGO City or Friends, $34.99

A $27 Little People box is not the sensible middle. It is a playset. So is $35 of LEGO. The chocolate box is the only row that is still just a countdown.

> Remember: The $2 box is the countdown. The $35 box is a toy.

You can also walk past the whole wall. Christmas morning is already a day. A cardboard countdown is a nice extra. It is not a second holiday you owe the house.

> Say: "If we do a calendar, it's the two-dollar one."

## Keep it out of the turkey cart

The dinner is a closed list. Walmart, Aldi, or Target already posted a number, and the move is to pick one and stop. That plan is in [the basket is the dinner](/guides/the-basket-is-the-dinner). A calendar is not on the dinner list. If it rides in the cart because you are already there, a posted meal grows a $35 toy.

Aldi's turkey price in that same November 4 ad starts the day the calendars do. Pie mix starts November 11. You may be standing there for the bird. The calendar wall is a different decision, made before you park, not at the endcap.

> Truth: A second box in the turkey cart is how $2 becomes $37.

> Say: "The basket is the dinner. The calendar stays the cheap one."

Write the number down the way you capped a school book fair. One number, decided before the table, is the whole trick in [the book fair is one number](/guides/the-book-fair-is-one-number). This number is easier. It is 2.

## Doors start in December

Advent 2026 starts Sunday, November 29. A 24-door calendar opened on December 1 runs through December 24. Bought the week of November 4, the box sits in a cabinet for most of a month.

That month is how "we might as well get the good one" starts. The good one is a gift. This aisle is not the gift list. You do not open a door on November 9 because the store stocked the box in November.

> Remember: A calendar bought in November lives in a closet until December.

If the LEGO box is what your kid asked for as a present, it can be the present. It does not also get to be the daily chocolate. One job.

> To your kid: "One chocolate box. Doors start in December."

If the answer is no on the LEGO calendar, keep it to one line. A longer no is in [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go). You do not owe a speech in the aisle.

> To your kid: "Not the LEGO one. We're doing chocolate doors."

## One box, then stop

The middle of the wall is the trap. Monster Jam at $24.99 feels reasonable next to LEGO at $34.99. It is still a toy price. Squishmallows at $29.99 are a plush gift with a hinge. Little People at $26.99 is a playset sold as a countdown.

Pick one:

- No calendar. Christmas morning still happens.
- The $1.99 chocolate box. Open it in December.
- A toy calendar only if it is the gift, and then you do not also buy the chocolate one.

Not two. This is one of those small yeses, and it is optional. The wider pattern is in [the dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax).

> Say: "One box. I'm not getting both."

These are Aldi Finds, in the store for a short time. Aldi does not restock them online after your store sells through. If the $1.99 box is gone, skip it. Do not upgrade to LEGO because the cheap one sold out. A missing $2 box is not an emergency.

## The point

Broke does not mean broken. A countdown can be two dollars of chocolate and a door a day. The LEGO calendar can be loved in somebody else's cart. Yours already has a dinner to cook.

> Remember: Decide on the calendar before you hit that aisle.
