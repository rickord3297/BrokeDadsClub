Football season is not one bill. It is a stack: streaming apps, the "we should go once" stadium itch, snack Sundays, and betting ads aimed at your kid during commercial breaks.

This is the hub. Four guides, four different jobs. Pick the one that matches the decision in front of you.

## Watch without stacking every app

Your local team on CBS or Fox is free with an antenna. Primetime is a different story. Name your stack before kickoff and cancel what you do not use on Monday.

Read: [NFL Sunday without a $1,000 streaming bill](/guides/nfl-sunday-without-a-thousand-dollar-stream)

## Stadium day vs vacation money

A family of four at an NFL game averages about **$1,475** in 2026 before merch. That is not a casual afternoon. Price it once. Compare it to something else you want. Then say yes or no out loud.

Read: [One NFL game for four is a second vacation](/guides/one-nfl-game-for-four-is-a-second-vacation)

## Home watch party that still feels like game day

Skip the nosebleeds. One theme snack, a paper bracket, antenna or one app, and a clear end time so Sunday does not eat the week.

Read: [NFL home watch party on a budget](/guides/nfl-home-watch-party-on-a-budget)

## Fantasy with your kid, no sportsbook

A free family league on your phone. No cash stakes. One sentence ready when the betting ad rolls: we pick players for fun, we do not bet money.

Read: [Fantasy Sunday with your kid](/guides/fantasy-sunday-with-your-kid-no-betting-app)

> Note: Streaming prices and stadium averages above are **2026 numbers**. Refresh before the next season.

## Do this today (5 minutes)

1. Check if your team airs free locally this Sunday.
2. If someone asked "can we go to a game," write the real all-in number once.
3. If the answer is couch, pick one snack and one start time.

Broke does not mean you never watch football. It means you refuse to pretend an optional stack is mandatory.
