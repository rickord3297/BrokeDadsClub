Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. The text usually lands before that. "Just bring dessert." Or "a pie if you can, and maybe a side."

You are not hosting. The house that is feeding people already has a closed list, and that list is in [the basket is the dinner](/guides/the-basket-is-the-dinner). Your job is the thing they named. Most years that thing is a pie.

## Two prices, one job

MoneyLion's Costco roundup on November 6, 2026 put the Kirkland Signature pumpkin pie at $5.99 in the warehouse and $6.80 for same-day delivery. It is 58 ounces, a big bakery pie, about 12 slices. That covers a real table.

Walmart.com, checked November 7, listed the Freshness Guaranteed 8-inch pumpkin pie, 22 ounces in a plastic tray, at $4.94, down from $5.93. A different 22-ounce box on the same site was still near $5.93. The smaller pie fits a smaller table, or it is the one you grab when you are not a warehouse member. Your store's tag can move a dollar. The job does not.

Either pie is the assignment. Not both. Not a pie plus the casserole you bake so a store crust does not look lazy.

> Remember: One pie is the assignment.

Match the pie to the room you are walking into. The $5.99 pie feeds more people. The $4.94 pie is enough for about six, and it is enough when that is the table. If the warehouse case is empty, buy the Walmart pie. Do not turn a sold-out $6 dessert into a baking project.

> Say: "We'll bring one pumpkin pie."

Text that line now. Buy the pie Tuesday or Wednesday of Thanksgiving week, when the case is stocked. A fresh bakery pie does not need to live in your fridge for two weeks. Costco warehouses are closed on Thanksgiving Day, so Wednesday is the last grab.

## The $200 box is not dessert

The same November 6 roundup lists Costco's Complete Gourmet Thanksgiving Turkey Dinner at $199.99. It serves about eight: a turkey breast, potatoes, gravy, mac and cheese, corn, green beans, yams, stuffing, cranberry, rolls, cobbler, and two pumpkin pies. Pre-order runs through November 13.

That box is a whole dinner. You were asked for dessert. A $6 pie in the cart is how "while I'm here" becomes $200 and two pies nobody assigned you.

> Truth: The $200 kit is a dinner. You were asked for a pie.

If you are the one feeding the house, this is the wrong list. Use the posted basket and stop. A guest does not also buy the host's meal.

> Say: "Not the dinner kit. Just the pie."

## "And maybe a side" is a second dinner

"Just bring dessert" is already doing a lot of work. "And maybe a side" is the second dinner. Rolls, a casserole, a veggie tray, and a 2-liter turn $6 into a grocery trip you did not plan, in a week when the regular cart still has to happen.

That regular week is still the pair-the-nights list in [the $47 grocery week](/guides/the-47-dollar-grocery-week). Thanksgiving dessert does not get to blow it.

If they already named a pie and a side, keep the pie and ask who has the side. You are not the backup kitchen. If they come back and say they really do need the green beans, then the green beans are the assignment and the pie belongs to someone else. One named thing. The wider pattern, small yeses that become a real bill, is the [dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax).

> To the group text: "We've got dessert. One pumpkin pie."

> Truth: A second dish is a second dinner.

A store pie is not a dare. Five or six dollars that feeds the table is a full contribution. Homemade is a hobby. It is not the cover charge for walking in.

## What you tell the kids

A cousin will talk about a pie from scratch. You do not owe a speech about money, or about who bakes.

> To your kid: "We're bringing the pie. That's our part."

If the expensive part is getting there, seats versus the car is a different cut, in [four seats or one car](/guides/four-seats-or-one-car). If you are not going, the short no is in [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go). Dessert is not how you make the trip make sense.

> To your kid: "Store pie still counts."

## Text it before you park

Do this before the bakery case talks you into a project.

- You are a guest: one pie. Costco at $5.99 if you are already a member and the table is big. Walmart near $5 if you are not.
- The text said pie and a side: keep the pie. Let someone else take the side.
- You are hosting: the basket is the dinner. Do not also assign yourself dessert on top of it.
- The $199.99 kit stays on the website. It is not your cart.

> Say: "One pie. I'm not cooking a second dinner."

## The point

Broke does not mean broken. A $5.99 pie, or a $4.94 one, is a real seat at the table. The second dish is how a text that said dessert becomes the meal you were not asked to host.

> Remember: Name the pie, then stop.
