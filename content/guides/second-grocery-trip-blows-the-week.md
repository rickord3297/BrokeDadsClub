A $47 week does not die in the produce aisle. It dies on Wednesday when someone opens the fridge, announces there is "nothing," and you drive back for milk.

You come home with milk, two drinks, a bag of something crunchy, and a receipt that makes you quiet in the driveway.

The full cart and dinners live in [the $47 grocery week](/guides/the-47-dollar-grocery-week). This page is the failure mode: the second trip.

## Why the second trip costs more than the gap

You are tired. Kids are with you or texting requests. The store is built for that mood.

- You came for **one** thing.
- You leave with **eight**.
- The eight were never in the meal plan, so they do not replace anything. They stack.

> Remember: Count items, not vibes. A second trip that unloads more than three things is a new cart, not a top-off.

## What usually triggers it

**No planned snack.** If the checklist did not name one snack, the house invents three.

**Monday did not cook extra.** Tuesday "leftovers" were a rumor. Now you are shopping angry.

**Milk or bread as the excuse.** Real need. Fake cart discipline.

**Gas station dinner guilt.** You promised a "real meal tomorrow" with no food in the house. Tomorrow becomes another trip. Pair with [gas station dinner](/guides/gas-station-dinner) so the bridge meal is honest.

## Rules that keep one trip

1. **Shop from a list with a snack line.** Print the [grocery-week checklist](/resources/grocery-week-checklist) if you want it on the fridge.
2. **Cook extra on purpose Monday.** Rice, pasta, or thighs that become tacos later.
3. **Stock one bridge meal** you will actually eat: frozen pizza, eggs and tortillas, beans and rice. Not a fantasy salad kit.
4. **If you must go back, set a hard cap:** three items, no aisle wandering, no "while we are here."

> Say: "Milk and bananas. That is the list."

## When the second trip is the right call

Kids are home sick. The power was out. You hosted three extra mouths. That is life, not failure.

The problem is the casual second trip that happens because the plan had no snack and no leftovers. That is [dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax) in grocery form: a small leak that feels reasonable every time it springs.

## The point

Shop once is not moral purity. It is how a tight week stays a tight week. Fix the trigger, not your character. The club is full of dads who learned this the expensive way, one "quick" receipt at a time.
