Parents are back in the comments arguing about the same fork: pay kids for every chore, or never pay because "family doesn't invoice."

Both pure versions break.

Pay for everything and you raise a tiny contractor who asks what wiping the table pays. Pay for nothing and you miss a clean way to practice real money without a lecture.

The hybrid version is boring on purpose.

## Two lists on the fridge

**House list (unpaid):** make your bed, clear your plate, put shoes where shoes go, help with a shared meal task, keep your room walkable. This is rent for living here. Not a side hustle.

**Job list (paid):** wash the car, bag donation clothes, weed the strip by the fence, deep-clean the bathroom once, help with a garage sort. Stuff you might otherwise pay a neighbor kid or skip.

If a task is on the house list, money does not enter the chat. If it is on the job list, name a price before the work starts. "We'll see" is how resentment gets overtime.

## A small base, if you use one

Some houses add a small weekly base so the kid has money to practice with even in a quiet week. That base is for belonging and budgeting practice, not for bribing toothbrushing.

Keep it small. A week of bad choices should feel real. A week of good choices should not require a refinance.

Pair the cash with a simple split: spend, save, give. Three jars beat an app pitch. For the talk that goes with the jars, use [talking to kids about money](/guides/talking-to-kids-about-money).

## What you say when they try to renegotiate breakfast

- "Breakfast is not a gig."
- "House list is free. Job list pays."
- "You want cash, pick a job from the list."
- "We don't tip for existing."

Say it once. Post the lists. Repeat as needed without a speech.

## The point

Kids need two lessons that are easy to smash together: you help because you live here, and money is tied to optional work you choose. The hybrid allowance keeps both. Broke does not mean broken. It means the chore chart is not a payroll, and the kid still gets a chance to hold real dollars without turning the kitchen into Uber Eats for towels.
