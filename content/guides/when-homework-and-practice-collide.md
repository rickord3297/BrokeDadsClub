September looked fine on paper. Then fall sports started. Practice moved to 5 p.m. Homework still exists. Dinner became a theory. By 8 p.m. you are negotiating fractions in a car that smells like shin guards.

This is not bad parenting. It is two schedules drawn by different people who never met.

> Remember: Collision is a calendar problem before it is a kid problem.

## One calendar, not three apps

School portal. Team group chat. Your work Outlook. None of them talk to each other.

Once a week, ten minutes, copy the non-negotiables to one place:

- Early release days
- Practice start and end (real end, not optimistic end)
- Tests or big projects you know about

You are not building NASA software. You are stopping the surprise Tuesday where practice and the science board due date share a birthday.

## The after-practice order

Same sequence most nights:

1. **Snack and water** (from [after-school collapse](/guides/the-after-school-collapse-is-not-a-bad-kid) rules)
2. **Shower if needed**
3. **One homework block, 30-45 minutes**
4. **Stop**

No car interrogation. No "how was practice" before food. Presence, then work.

> Truth: Hungry kids do not do math. They perform despair.

## Triage when time runs out

Pick one must-do:

- The assignment due tomorrow
- The reading that takes ten minutes but counts
- The teacher who actually checks

Everything else waits or gets an email: "We had a late game; we'll finish Thursday."

Teachers often say yes if you ask before midnight panic.

## When the coach schedule changes late

Happens every season. A text at 4 p.m. moves practice. Your brain explodes.

Default response: adjust dinner, shrink homework to the must-do, bed on time anyway. Sleep beats a perfect worksheet.

Ask the coach once per season for stable end times in writing. Some will, some will not. You still plan from reality, not hope.

## Big projects vs game nights

If [whose science project is it](/guides/whose-science-project-is-it) lands the same week as two games, split work across earlier days. Poster work on Sunday, not Wednesday after a doubleheader.

That is [dropping one activity](/guides/dropping-one-activity-so-the-week-can-breathe) logic applied to an evening, not a season.

## Scripts

> To your kid: "Snack first. One homework page. Then we stop."

> To the teacher: "Late practice tonight; we'll turn in the math sheet tomorrow morning."

> To the coach: "What time should parents plan for pickup most nights?"

## The point

When homework and practice collide, stop trying to be two schedules at once. One calendar, snack before math, triage the must-dos, protect sleep. Fall sports is a season, not a permanent velocity. Broke does not mean broken. It means you pick the week you can actually finish.
