October shows up with a costume question before you have finished the first school month. Then it shows up again. School parade. Trunk-or-treat. The block on the 31st. Each one can sound like a new character if you let the week talk you into it.

The National Retail Federation's 2026 Halloween survey put planned spending around $115 a person. Families with kids were the group most likely to pull last year's bin instead of buying fresh. That is the move. Not a lecture about holidays. A plan you can say out loud in the car.

> Remember: Three events is not three costumes.

## Count the nights before you count the characters

Write the real list on a sticky note before anyone opens a browser:

- School party or parade (daytime, indoors, maybe forty minutes)
- Trunk-or-treat or daycare (often a week early, and someone will decorate a car)
- Halloween night on the block

That is one kid, maybe two, wearing the same outfit more than once. The damage is rarely the main piece. It is the extra wig, the boots that only match this one idea, and the candy bucket you already own in the garage.

Same pattern as [birthday party math](/guides/birthday-party-math). The theme is not the budget. The number of add-ons is.

If a family group costume is actually fun for you (Scooby, a sports team, whatever the kid pitched at dinner), do it as one shared bit. One matching hat can be the group. Three licensed jumpsuits is a second grocery week wearing a mask.

## Name the cap in the driveway

Pick a number before the aisle. For a lot of houses, $20-$30 covers the kid's costume when you already own a black shirt, a hoodie, or face paint. Candy gets its own small number, maybe $15, bought once and not "while we are here." Decorations you already own count as this year's decor.

Say the cap where your partner can hear it, before the store, not in the costume aisle with a six-year-old negotiating.

> To your partner: "Cap is $25 for the costume. Candy is its own $15."

> To your kid: "One costume. You pick it. We wear it more than once."

If they freeze, give two choices you can actually build. Hoodie plus mask, or last year's outfit plus one new piece. Open-ended "what do you want to be" in a pop-up shop is how the cap dies.

## Make one outfit do the week

Versions that survive a school gym and a cold sidewalk:

- A hoodie you own, plus a cheap mask or face paint
- Last year's costume with one new piece (a cape, a hat, a plastic tool)
- A character from clothes that already fit: referee, chef, construction, athlete

Cold weather is not a second costume. Fleece tights, a long-sleeve shirt, and boots go under the outfit. A coat worn open still shows the character. Buying one size up so layers fit is cheaper than a backup outfit for a 38-degree night.

For a baby, skip the cheap fluffy costumes with long strands. They shed, and a kid will chew them. A plain printed sleeper is a costume and pajamas. You already paid for clothes.

> Truth: The kid remembers who walked the block with them.

## When they want the $40 character

Sometimes the store costume is the right yes. Fine. Then that is the costume. It goes to the school parade and it goes out on the 31st. You do not add a nicer one for pictures, and you do not buy a "backup" in case this one feels silly by week three.

If it is over the cap, sleep on it. One night is enough. A lot of licensed pieces get cheaper closer to the week, and a thrift cape plus a paper mask gets most of the photo.

> Say: "We can do that one. It is the only one."

> Or: "That one is over what we set. Pick again, or we build it Friday."

If the real want is "I do not want to look wrong next to the other kids," keep it short. That is the same muscle as [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go). Specific, kind, done. Not a speech about prices.

## What not to buy in the same trip

Leave these for a different day, or skip them:

- A second costume "just for school"
- Yard inflatables because the neighbor already has three
- A matching family set you will wear for forty minutes
- Candy you will rebuy because the bag got opened on a Tuesday

Your bin in the garage is part of the budget. Most families already reuse at least half their costume pieces, and even more of the decor. Using it is not a downgrade. It is how October stays one line item instead of a pile of small yeses.

This is [the dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax) in October clothes. One costume is a clean yes. The accessories aisle is where a normal week tips, the same way an extra errand blows a grocery plan.

## The point

Broke does not mean broken, and a reused cape is not a lesser Halloween. Pick the outfit once. Wear it to the parade, the parking lot, and the block. Then go home before the pop-up shop convinces you the night needed a wig.
