Someone in the group chat just dropped $90 on cleats. You are on your phone in the parking lot scrolling Marketplace at 10 p.m., feeling like a cheapskate because the listing says "worn twice."

You are not dumpster diving. You are skipping retail markup on gear that outgrows a kid before the laces break in. That is not shame. That is supply chain literacy with a porch pickup.

> Remember: Re-use shame is about status. A clean used bike that fits is about function.

## What Marketplace is actually for

Marketplace is not a garage sale personality test. It is where normal families list the dresser, bike, and sport bag they replaced last month.

**Buy used here:**
- Cleats, pads, bats, helmets you can inspect (see the never list below)
- Bikes, scooters, wagons
- Dressers, bookshelves, kid desks
- Strollers and gear with visible date stamps
- Tools, camp chairs, the wagon you need once a season

**Buy new or skip:**
- Car seats (expiry, crash history you cannot verify)
- Helmets with mystery dents or no label
- Anything missing safety hardware
- Mattresses (just no)

> Truth: Used is a category decision, not a moral grade.

This pairs with [thrift without looking like a dare](/guides/thrift-without-looking-like-a-dare) for clothes and [last year's shoes in a new hallway](/guides/last-years-shoes-in-a-new-hallway) for what you already own. Marketplace is the middle path: you need the thing, you do not need the box.

## The ten-minute checklist

Before you message anyone:

1. **Search exact size, brand, model.** "Soccer cleats size 3" beats browsing "kids shoes."
2. **Filter local pickup, posted in the last week.** Stale listings waste time.
3. **Read photos like a skeptic.** Pits, soles, cracks, missing pieces. No hero shot only.
4. **Message one question:** "Any cracks, missing pieces, or smells I should know about?"
5. **Meet in daylight, public place.** Library parking lot, grocery store, police station lobby if your town has one.
6. **Bring a friend for big loads.** Dressers and bikes are two-person math.
7. **Pay after you inspect.** Cash or Venmo on the spot. No deposits to "hold" it.

If the seller pushes weird, you walk. Walking is free.

## One sane win

Store cleats: $80. Marketplace listing from a kid one grade up: $25, worn twice, outgrown by November. You saved $55 and a Target run. You did not start a side hustle. You bought shoes.

Same math for the [sports signup fee](/guides/the-sports-signup-fee-you-didnt-budget-for) pile: registration is fixed, gear is negotiable if you shop used first.

## Beat the shame at home

**To your partner:**

> "Same item, half price, meet at the library. If it is junk we walk."

**To your kid:**

> "We are getting you the thing. We are not paying for the box."

**To yourself in the pickup line:**

> "Would I buy this if nobody was watching?"

You do not owe strangers a speech about your budget. One sentence beats an apology tour, same spine as [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go).

## When new still wins

If safety is fuzzy, buy new. If time is zero and retail is $12, buy new. If your kid needs one status item this season, pair it with used everything else. Pride in one choice is fine. Pride in every receipt is expensive.

## Scripts

> To the seller: "Can I pick up Saturday at the library lot? I will inspect and pay then."

> To another parent: "Got cleats off Marketplace. Kid outgrows them anyway."

> To the kid who wanted the mall: "Yours work. We spent the rest on groceries."

## The point

Marketplace before the mall is not a dare. It is how stretched dads buy gear that still has six months of life without funding a shame spiral. Search with a list, meet in daylight, inspect like you mean it, and say it like a normal Tuesday. Broke does not mean broken. It means you know where the barely-used cleats live.
