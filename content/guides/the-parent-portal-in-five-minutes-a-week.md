Your kid's school runs on an app you have not opened since August. Grades, missing work, field trip forms, and the thing due tomorrow all live in there. You do not need to become the homework cop. You need a five-minute habit so nothing blindsides you in October.

Principals keep saying the same thing this back-to-school season: parents who peek at the portal weekly catch small problems before they become parent-teacher conference emergencies. Dads get sidelined on school comms all the time. The portal is one place you can show up without asking anyone's permission.

## Do this today

1. **Find the portal.** Search your email for the school's app name or ask your kid to log in once while you watch.
2. **Bookmark it.** Phone home screen or browser bar. Friction is why you stop checking.
3. **Set a Sunday reminder.** Seven p.m., five minutes, "portal." Same time every week.

If pickup small talk is still awkward, [the pickup line is not a networking event](/guides/the-pickup-line-is-not-a-networking-event) has lines. This is the digital version of showing up.

## What to look at (and what to skip)

**Look at:**
- Missing assignments (usually a red dot or zero)
- One class that dropped a letter grade in two weeks
- Calendar for the next seven days

**Skip:**
- Refreshing every hour
- Commenting on every post like a fan account
- Reading every rubric at 11 p.m.

> Remember: The portal is early warning, not a surveillance camera.

## The five-minute Sunday routine

1. Open the app.
2. Check missing work across classes. Note one name if something is overdue.
3. Glance at grades. Anything new below a C or a sudden drop?
4. Scan the calendar for permission slips, early release, picture day.
5. Close the app.

If something needs action, message the teacher with one specific question. Not a novel.

> "Hi, I am [name], [kid]'s dad. I see a missing assignment in math from Tuesday. Is there a copy online or should they ask in class?"

Short. Polite. Done.

## Scripts at home

Kid: "Why are you checking my grades?"

You:

> "I check once a week so we fix small stuff early. I am not reading every answer."

Kid: "Mom already does that."

You:

> "Good. I am on the list too now. Two adults paying attention beats one person drowning."

## When to actually worry

One missing worksheet is normal the first month. A pattern across multiple classes for three weeks is a conversation. Start with the teacher, not a lecture in the car.

If evenings already detonate at pickup, read [the after-school collapse is not a bad kid](/guides/the-after-school-collapse-is-not-a-bad-kid) before you add portal stress on top of hunger and tired.

## The point

You do not need to be the parent who knows every quiz score. You need to be the parent who is not surprised. Five minutes on Sunday, one bookmark, one calm question when it matters. That is enough to count as involved.
