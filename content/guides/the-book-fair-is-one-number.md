The flyer comes home in the same pile as the costume note and the class party sheet. This one has a graphic novel, a poster, and a bin of erasers that look like candy. Your kid will walk the library during the school day. You will not be in that aisle unless you go back at night.

Penn Valley Elementary in Lower Merion told families the fair runs October 19 through 22, 2026, and suggested $20 to $25 per child. Felton Laboratory Charter School in Orangeburg posted a fair for October 26 through 30, 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. You are not the only house holding this flyer. It is not a personal test.

> Remember: The book fair is a cap, not a wish list.

## Put the number in before the library

On the fall 2026 elementary flyer, items run about $2.99 to $15.99. An exclusive paperback sits near $9.99. A graphic novel can land around $14.99. A hardcover on that same flyer is $15.99. Two "just this one" books can spend a $25 cap before anybody touches an eraser.

That is the whole plan. One number. Books first. Toys only if something is left. Write it on the envelope, or load it in a Scholastic eWallet, the night before. A kid who knows the number can shop. A kid holding a debit card is shopping the whole table.

Penn Valley listed three ways to pay, and put the suggested budget right next to them: cash, an eWallet, or a credit or debit card sent in with the student. The card is the one with no ceiling once they are in line. Skip it.

> Truth: The eraser aisle is how the book disappears.

## Cash and the eWallet are not the same leftover

Some fairs are coinless. Penn Valley said any change from dollars goes to Share the Fair, the pot that lets another student shop. If you send a $20 bill and the book is $9.99, you did not get $10 back. You funded the pot. That can be a fine choice. It is a bad surprise.

An eWallet is the other leftover. Scholastic's terms say those funds are not redeemed for cash. After the fair, what your kid did not spend stays in the parent account. You can use it at a later fair, move it, or shop the Scholastic store. Some schools also say balances under $1 get donated. Read the note from your school before you load $25 and assume the change comes home in a pocket.

If you want the number to be exact, load the eWallet with the cap, or send bills that match a book you already circled. Do not send a twenty and call the missing ten a lesson you meant to teach.

> Say: "You've got $20. Books first. If it's not enough, we put it back."

## The night before is one rule

You do not need a speech about publishing margins. You need the number, and you need books before toys. Putting something back is allowed.

> To your kid: "If two books don't fit, we keep the one you'll open."

If they come home with one book and a story about the poster they left, the cap worked. The library has the second title. You do not owe the flyer a second trip.

If the kid at the next desk walks out with a stack, that is not a bill. It is somebody else's number.

> Say: "Their envelope was different. Yours was $20."

> Truth: Somebody else's stack is not your receipt.

## The night shift is a separate yes

A lot of fairs add a family night. Penn Valley's is PJs and Cookie Night on October 22, 6 to 8 p.m., plus a volunteer ask for the shopping shift and the cookies. That is three jobs wearing one flyer.

You already know this move from the [class party signup](/guides/the-class-party-signup-is-one-job). One slot, or a short no. The book budget and the evening are not a package.

> Say: "I can send the envelope. I can't stay for the table."

If you do go at night, bring the same number. A family night is how a $20 cap becomes a graphic novel, a poster, and a cookie you also baked.

## If your fair already happened

Some schools ran the fair last week. The number still matters for the school down the road, and for spring. Write it where you will see it while the flyer is still on the counter. October does not have to teach your kid that every sheet from school is a promise.

The [school supply list](/guides/school-supply-list) was the same shape in August. A list with no cap spends the month. A list with a number spends an afternoon.

## The point

Broke does not mean broken. Circle one book if you want. Load one number. Skip the card. Let them put something back. You did not fail the fair because the poster stayed on the table.
