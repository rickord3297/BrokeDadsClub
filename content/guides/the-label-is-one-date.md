December 3 is a Thursday. Hanukkah starts tomorrow. The counter will still offer three speeds for a box that does not need three speeds.

You need one address and one date. Write the date on the box before you look at the menu.

## Tomorrow is not a mailbox day

The USPS holiday shipping page says Hanukkah runs December 4 through December 12 this year, and it tells you to adjust. The Christmas dates on that page will not land a gift for the first night.

If the present is not already in your house, a label printed tonight does not put it in someone else's house on Friday. Buy it in town. Or hand over something you already have. That is a finished gift. A tracking number is not.

> Remember: A box mailed today does not arrive tomorrow.

The same page lists Kwanzaa as December 26 through January 1, and it uses the December 25 send-by dates for those gifts. That one can ride the later deadline below. Hanukkah cannot.

## Wednesday is the date that is still open

For a gift leaving the country, USPS recommends Wednesday, December 9, for First-Class Package International and for Priority Mail International. That date is the same for every region on the page, including Canada, Mexico, and Europe. Priority Mail Express International is Wednesday, December 16. You do not need that one on December 3. You still have the 9th.

Military and diplomatic mail is the same week, with one service already gone. On the USPS page, Ground Advantage to the APO, FPO, and DPO ZIP groups it lists was Wednesday, November 4. That window closed. First-Class Mail and Priority Mail are Wednesday, December 9, for ZIPs 090-092, 093, 094-099, 340, and 962-966. Priority Mail Express Military, where a post office even offers it, is Wednesday, December 16. ZIP 093 does not list that express service. Ask before you pay for it.

USPS says the real day still depends on the two ZIPs and when the counter takes the box. Write the recommended date anyway.

> Truth: The cheap military box had to leave on November 4. December 9 is the date you still have.

One box, with the name, rank, and unit the clerk asks for. A second box is a second errand. [The list is one name](/guides/the-list-is-one-name) already covered the gift.

## The lower 48 can wait until the 17th

USPS posted the domestic dates on September 22, 2026, and the holiday page still matches that release. For the contiguous United States, expected delivery before December 25:

- USPS Ground Advantage: Thursday, December 17
- First-Class Mail: Thursday, December 17
- Priority Mail: Friday, December 18
- Priority Mail Express: Saturday, December 19

Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, and the territories are not that list. Ground Advantage to and from those places is 10 or more days, and the page says to check the ZIP. First-Class Mail there is still December 17. Do not assume the 17th is safe for a heavy Ground Advantage box to Honolulu. Look up the ZIP, then write that date.

Retail prices are from USPS Notice 123, effective October 4, 2026, at the counter:

- Ground Advantage, up to 8 ounces: $8.40 in zone 1 and $10.20 in zone 8
- Ground Advantage, 1 pound: $10.05 in zone 1 and $13.65 in zone 8
- Priority Mail Small Flat Rate Box: $14.65
- Priority Mail Express Flat Rate Envelope: $38.25

Zone 1 is close. Zone 8 is the far side of the lower 48. Your receipt prints the zone. The shape does not change. A light gift is about $8 to $14. The Express envelope is $38.25, and it buys two days past the Ground Advantage date.

> Remember: Express is a late fee. It is not a better gift.

If you can get to the counter on December 17, you already bought the time. The flat-rate envelope is for a box you held too long. On December 3, you have not held it too long.

## Write the date, then the label

Overseas, or an APO that missed November 4: write December 9. Lower 48, Christmas or Kwanzaa: write December 17. Hanukkah, first night: do not write a date. Go to the store.

> Say: "Ground Advantage. December 17."

If the clerk points at Express and your date is still open:

> Say: "The 17th works. I'll keep the slower one."

If the address is overseas and they offer the December 16 express price a week early:

> Say: "December 9. Not the express."

> To your kid: "It ships this week. They open it at their house."

If you are not shipping one, say that in the same breath. A short no is the same muscle as [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go).

> Say: "We're not mailing one. We'll see them."

That is a complete decision. The calendar had a date. You used it, or you skipped the line.

## The point

Broke doesn't mean broken. One address gets one date. Tomorrow's Hanukkah gift stays in town. Wednesday, December 9, covers overseas mail and the military services that are still open. Thursday, December 17, covers Ground Advantage in the lower 48. Leave the $38.25 envelope on the rack.
