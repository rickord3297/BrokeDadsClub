The signup email lands, and ten minutes later the gear emails follow. Cleats, a "recommended" bag, grip socks, a training net for the backyard. The total quietly passes the registration fee.

Here is the honest list. For a first rec season, four things before practice one. Everything else waits until you know your kid still likes it after two weeks. If you have not run the full season number yet, start with the [youth sports all-in cost checklist](/guides/youth-sports-all-in-cost-checklist).

## The short answer

Buy **cleats, shin guards, a ball, and a water bottle**. That is about $60-$90 at normal prices. Most rec leagues include the jersey, shorts, and socks in the fee, so read the email before you buy any of those.

> Remember: The fee is the down payment. Gear is where the season gets expensive, one reasonable purchase at a time.

## Essential equipment: buy before practice one

### Cleats

Molded rubber studs, not metal. Most youth leagues ban metal anyway. Fit matters more than brand: a thumb's width of room at the toe is growing room, more than that is a blister.

```gear-pick
url: https://www.amazon.com/s?k=youth+molded+soccer+cleats
tip: Try them on with the shin guards and game socks on. That is the real fit.
```

### Shin guards

Required in nearly every league, and the one item coaches actually check. Slip-in guards with an ankle sleeve stay put better on small legs than the strap-only kind.

```gear-pick
title: Youth shin guards with ankle sleeves
verdict: essential
targetPrice: Under $15
merchant: amazon
url: https://www.amazon.com/s?k=youth+soccer+shin+guards+ankle+sleeve
tip: Size by your kid's height on the back of the package, not by age.
```

### A ball in the right size

**Size 3** for under 8, **size 4** for 9 to 12, **size 5** from 13 up. The wrong size is not a small thing: a size 5 at age seven is a bowling ball. Some leagues hand one out at the first practice, so ask before you buy.

### A water bottle they can open alone

Big enough for a full practice, simple enough that a seven-year-old can open it without a parent. Write the name on it with a paint pen. Unlabeled bottles are a donation to the field.

## Unnecessary upsells (skip these in season one)

### Team-branded everything

The logo bag, the matching hoodie, the car decal. Nice when the season is year three. Optional in year one, whatever the order form implies.

### Backyard training gear

Rebounder nets, agility ladders, cone sets. Practice twice a week is plenty for a first-year player. If they start asking to practice at home, a wall and the ball you already own does the job.

### Specialty socks and gloves

Grip socks and keeper gloves solve problems a beginner does not have yet. Gloves wait until a coach actually puts your kid in goal.

> Truth: Gear does not make a kid like a sport. Playing time and a ride home where nobody critiques them do.

## Timing purchases

### Before practice one

The four essentials. Nothing else.

### After two weeks

This is the cutoff. If they are still excited after two weeks of practice, buy the next layer: a bag with a separate shoe pocket, a second ball for home. Kids quit things. That is not a parenting failure, it is being eight.

### End of season

Clearance racks and league swap tables are where next year's cleats come from. Buy one size up only if the price is too good to ignore, and write the size on the box.

> Say: "We buy the rest if you still love it in two weeks."

## Storage tips

### One bin by the door

Everything soccer lives in one bin: cleats, guards, ball, bottle. If it is not in the bin, it does not exist, and you are not searching the house at 8:40 on a Saturday.

### Dry the cleats

Knock the mud off outside, stuff them with newspaper, and leave them out of the bag overnight. Cleats that live wet in a bag fall apart by October and smell like it by September.

### The size-up shelf

Outgrown gear goes on one shelf with the size written on masking tape. Younger siblings, cousins, or the league swap table. Gear that fits nobody in the house goes back out instead of into a closet.

## The point

A first season costs what you let it. Four things up front, a cutoff date on your calendar, and a bin by the door. Buy the rest for the kid who is still showing up after two weeks, not the one in the gear catalog.
