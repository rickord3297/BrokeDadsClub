"We're broke this weekend" often becomes "everyone dissolve into tablets." Screens are a tool. They are a terrible only plan. Kids can smell when the weekend had no shape.

You do not need money to have a shape. You need a short list and a start time. Same calm as [cheap date night](/guides/cheap-date-night): change the rules, keep the people.

## The rule of three

Pick **three** anchors before Saturday morning:

1. **One out:** walk, park, library, free museum hour, church lawn, trail, grocery store as "field trip."
2. **One make:** fort, pancakes, cardboard spaceship, sidewalk chalk city, bake the cheap cookies.
3. **One rest:** movie, books on the couch, quiet hour that you also take.

Write them on a sticky note. Stick it where people can see it. Now the weekend has a spine.

## Free / nearly free outs

- Library (story time, comics, air conditioning)
- Park with a ball you already own
- Window-shopping a hardware store like it is a museum
- Visit the same pond / creek / big tree and call it tradition
- Help a neighbor for an hour if that fits your life

## Make-at-home that does not require a craft blog

- Blanket fort + flashlight
- "Restaurant night" with paper menus and food you already have
- Bike repair clinic in the driveway
- Sort the Lego / toy bin as a game with a timer
- Write letters to grandparents

## Screens with a fence

If screens are happening, put a fence on them: after the out, or after the make, or one movie Saturday night. Unlimited scrolling is how a free weekend still feels expensive in regret.

## Scripts

- "We're home this weekend on purpose. Here's the plan."
- "Bored is allowed for ten minutes. Then pick from the sticky note."
- "Screens after the park, not instead of the park."

When they push for the expensive version, keep it short like [explaining “we can’t go”](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go). One honest plan beats a guilt speech.

## What not to do

Do not apologize for a home weekend like you failed a test. Do not schedule seventeen activities and burn out by noon. Do not buy your way out of boredom with a mall trip you cannot afford.

## The point

Cheap weekends are not punishment. They are reps, teaching kids that fun can be invented. Broke does not mean broken. It means you still show up with a sticky note and a ball.
