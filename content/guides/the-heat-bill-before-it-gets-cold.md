The first cold night is a terrible time to meet your heat bill. By then the tank is low, the price is whatever it is, and someone is already cold.

If you heat with oil, the number in the news this week is not small. The National Energy Assistance Directors Association says the average oil-heat household is looking at about $2,627 this winter, up from about $1,749 last winter. That is roughly $878 more, about 50 percent. New England is closer to $1,000 more. Outlets including USA Today carried it in the first days of October 2026. It is a forecast, not your invoice. It is still the kind of number you want on paper before the truck shows up.

If you heat with gas or electric, ignore the oil headline and keep the homework. Last January's bill is the real number. Summer usage is a decoy.

> Remember: An unnamed heat bill becomes a fight. A named one becomes a plan.

## Find last winter's number this week

You need one figure, not a spreadsheet.

- Oil: last winter's total, or last year's gallons times a current price if the company will quote it.
- Gas or electric: last January, plus December if January was a weird month.
- Rent with heat included: you are done. Say that out loud so nobody stress-buys a space heater that quietly becomes the electric bill.

Write it on a sticky note. Round it. "$1,800 last year" is more useful than a reconstruction project at 10 p.m.

If you cannot find the bill, call and ask for last winter's total. That is a normal request. You are not confessing anything.

## One call before the tank is empty

Empty is when you lose your options. Full price, a rush, and a cold kid can land on the same afternoon.

Ask for one of these, whichever they actually offer:

- A budget plan that spreads the season
- A partial fill so you are not buying a whole winter on the worst week
- A payment date that matches your paycheck, not the day the truck feels like it

> Say: "Can we set a budget plan, or a smaller fill, before the tank is empty?"

Write down what they said. "They were nice" is not a plan. "Partial fill Thursday, $400" is.

If the quote is ugly, you still learned it on a Tuesday you chose. That beats learning it when the heat dies during dinner.

## The talk with your partner

Do this before someone turns the thermostat into a personality test.

> To your partner: "Last winter was about $X. I want one number we can live with before we order."

Pick the number together. If oil is your fuel, start from last year and add a serious chunk. The public forecast is about half again, not a rounding error. If that new number does not fit, the next sentence is which rooms stay warm, not who is bad with money.

> Remember: A cold house is not a character test.

Same energy as [explaining we can't go](/guides/explaining-we-cant-go): short, kind, decided. Two speeches make it worse.

If this bill lands in the same month as a car repair or daycare, use [fixing the car vs paying for daycare](/guides/car-vs-daycare) and pick the lesser disaster on purpose. Heat is usually the one you cannot skip. Name what moves instead.

## If the number does not fit

A few moves that are not "wear a coat inside and like it":

- Close rooms you do not use after dinner. Bedrooms and the room where you actually sit stay in the plan.
- Ask about energy assistance. Search your state plus LIHEAP. Rules are local. One application. You do not owe the sideline a story.
- Skip the extra space heater until you know what it does to the electric bill. A heater can become a second heat bill.
- Put the next fill on the calendar so empty is not the reminder.

> Truth: The truck does not care that you meant to look in November.

This sits in the same family as [the dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax): a cost that shows up because people live in the house. It is not proof you failed October.

## What you tell the kids

They notice a colder room. They notice when you and your partner go quiet after the mail.

> To your kid: "We stay warm where we sleep and where we sit. We are choosing rooms, not choosing you."

Skip the dollar amount unless you already talk about money that way. If you do, keep it plain, the way [talking to kids about money](/guides/talking-to-kids-about-money) says: facts, not fear.

## The point

Broke does not mean broken. Look up last winter before the first cold week. Make one call. Tell your partner the number while everyone is still warm enough to think. The bill is allowed to be big. It is not allowed to be a surprise that lands on the kids.
