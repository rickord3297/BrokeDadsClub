"We can't go" is a complete sentence. Kids will ask why. You do not owe a board meeting.

The goal is honesty without horror, enough truth that they trust you, not so much detail that they start managing the household budget at age eight. For a deeper age-by-age version, read [talking to kids about money](/guides/talking-to-kids-about-money).

## The short script

1. **The no:** "We're not going to that one."
2. **The reason (one line):** "It's not in our plan this month." / "We're saving for home stuff first."
3. **The door:** "Want to help pick something we *can* do instead?"

That is the whole speech. Stop talking.

## Age-size the why

**Little kids:** "We buy food and our house first. Then fun. This is a food-and-house week."

**School age:** "We have a money plan. The plan says yes to the park and the library. It says not this month to the big ticket thing."

**Teens:** "Here's the shape: rent, food, gas, then leftover. Leftover isn't there for this. You're not the problem. The calendar is."

## Phrases that help

- "Not this time" (leaves room for later without promising)
- "That's a want. We're covering needs first."
- "I wish we could. We can't this round."

## Phrases that hurt

- "Because of you / your brother / your mother"
- "We're broke" said like a threat
- A twenty-minute inflation lecture
- "Don't tell anyone"

Fear and secrecy are not money education. Calm limits are.

## Offer a real alternative

A no with nothing after it feels like a wall. A no with a walk, a movie night, a friend at the park, or a homemade version of the expensive thing feels like parenting. Party FOMO? See [birthday party math](/guides/birthday-party-math).

You are allowed to be disappointed too. Feel it away from the kid if you can. Then come back and be the adult who can say no without collapsing.

## The point

"We can't go" does not mean "we are failing." It means you are steering. Broke does not mean broken, and a short, kind no is one of the most expensive-looking skills a dad can have for free.
