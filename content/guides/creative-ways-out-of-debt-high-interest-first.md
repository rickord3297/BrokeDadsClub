Minimum payments are a treadmill. The bank loves them. You are still running at 11 p.m. wondering why the balance barely moves.

There is a boring name for the fix: avalanche. Pay minimums on everything. Throw every extra dollar at the highest interest rate until that account is dead. Then roll that payment to the next highest. No magic. Just math that actually finishes.

## Step one: the ugly list

One page. Every debt:

| Account | Balance | Min payment | Interest rate |
|---------|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Card A  | $4,200  | $85         | 24.99%        |
| Card B  | $900    | $35         | 19.99%        |
| Car     | $8,500  | $310        | 6.9%          |

Sort by interest rate, highest on top. That top line is your target. Not the biggest balance. Not the one that stresses you out most. The expensive one.

> Remember: Interest is the real bill. Principal is just the story it rides on.

## The avalanche in plain English

1. Pay minimums on all accounts. On time. Every month. Non-negotiable.
2. Find any extra cash (see below). Send it all to the top-rate account.
3. When that account hits zero, close it or freeze it in a drawer.
4. Take the old minimum plus the extra you were sending and aim it at the next highest rate.
5. Repeat until the list is short enough to fit on a Post-it.

A $50 extra payment on a 24% card saves more than $50 on a 7% car loan. That is the whole sermon.

## Creative cash that is not a second job

You do not need a 4 a.m. side hustle. You need small leaks plugged and one-time bumps steered to the target account.

**Bill swap week.** Cancel one recurring thing for 30 days (second streaming service, unused gym, the app you forgot). Put the exact amount to the highest-rate debt the day the bill would have hit. One month, one win.

**Sell one thing.** The extra drill, the bike nobody rides, the stack of baby gear in the garage. One Facebook Marketplace sale, one payment. Done.

**Tax refund / bonus rule.** Half to the target debt, half to life. Not zero fun. Not all fun.

**Call for a lower rate.** Credit card companies sometimes drop APR if you ask. Script:

> "I have been a customer for X years. I am working on paying this down. Can you lower my rate or match a balance transfer offer?"

They say no. You lose five minutes. They say yes. You save hundreds.

**Employer or credit union programs.** Some offer low-rate personal loans to pay off cards. Read the fine print. If the rate is actually lower and you cut up the cards, it can work.

**The pantry week.** Not a stunt. One week of [the $47 grocery week](/guides/the-47-dollar-grocery-week) and send the difference to debt. Pair with [the dad tax](/guides/the-dad-tax) so you know where the leaks are.

For cash that does not steal bedtime, filter gigs through [side hustles that don't steal bedtime](/guides/side-hustles-that-dont-steal-bedtime). One Saturday morning beats five weeknights away from home.

## When two bills fight (again)

Some months the car and daycare both scream. You already know that drill from [car vs daycare](/guides/car-vs-daycare). During a crisis week, go back to minimums everywhere and survive. The avalanche resumes when the fire is out. Progress is not linear. It is directional.

## Snowball if you need a win

If the highest-rate account is huge and you are losing heart, pay off the smallest balance first for momentum, then switch back to rate order. The perfect method you quit is worse than the good enough method you finish.

> Truth: Done beats optimized. Optimized and abandoned is just guilt with a spreadsheet.

## The point

High-interest debt is a subscription to being tired. Kill the expensive line first and the whole list gets lighter faster than minimums alone.

You are not bad with money. You are a dad paying interest on top of groceries, cleats, and the school fee that appeared in August. Pick one target. Hit it until it is gone. Then pick the next.

Broke does not mean broken. It means you stopped feeding the most expensive bill first.
